This week's report on the paid-API and MCP ecosystem opened with a number built for travel: 39.9% of the 134,916 paid APIs and MCP servers ToolVet checks failed their latest check, up from 12.8% the week before. Read at speed, it says two in five services in the market where agents pay per call are broken. The report ships its own JSON under CC BY 4.0, so before repeating the sentence I pulled every week that exists — three of them — and took it apart.

The first split is the headline letting one cohort speak for two. 50.3% of the paid x402 APIs (the per-call HTTP payment protocol behind the directory) failed their latest check; 11.2% of the remote MCP servers did. The MCP side has said almost the same thing for three weeks running: 9.6%, 10.9%, 11.2%. Whatever moved, moved on the paid-API side, and the headline names both cohorts in one breath.

The same file then answers whether anything is actually down. Of the 2,009,167 checks ToolVet ran that week, 18.3% failed, and the median hour of checking passed 91.8% of its runs, barely different from the two weeks before (91.2, 91.3). Across the three weeks, the share of all checks that failed was 15.1%, 10.8%, 18.3%, against the snapshots' 37.2, 12.8, 39.9. The snapshot nearly matches the run stream in the calm week and more than doubles it in the loud ones, and the mechanism is printed in plain text on the methodology page: verified, featured and actively used services are checked every 30 minutes, others every few hours, and "services that keep failing less often," with "each listing counts with its latest check in it." Passes stay fresh; a failing status keeps aging until something rechecks it, and each counts the same in the snapshot. A latest-check aggregate on that schedule is an accumulator of stale failures, and the directory keeps feeding it: the checker ran a flat 2.01 million checks each week while listings grew 65% in two weeks, so checks per listing fell from 24.6 to 14.9. The more the directory grows, the staler the average failure.

Reachability barely moved. The report's own counters sort 47,544 of the 53,779 failures as invalid (the endpoint answered, but not in the way that passes: a valid payment challenge for a paid API) and 3,464 as unreachable, with the remaining 2,771 being MCP servers that answered with an HTTP error or failed their handshake; unreachable has been flat for three weeks (2,806, 3,093, 3,464) while invalid swung 25.9K, 8.3K, 47.5K. The cohort-age story doesn't hold either: the directory added 23.1K, 26.1K and 27.2K listings in those weeks, so "the population grew 25%" is not this week's event but the directory's steady state, and nearly the same inflow produced a 12.8% headline a week earlier. The series is a sawtooth (37.2, 12.8, 39.9) over a fleet whose typical-moment health never moved.

The last split is the one I would want before repeating the number anywhere: 43,207 of the 98,778 paid APIs show any usage at all in 30 days, which leaves 55,571 with no recorded usage, and 625 show ten or more paying wallets. The report does not split its 50.3% by payer count, so whether the slice agents actually pay for was up is exactly the question the number cannot answer. I re-checked no endpoints myself; every number here is arithmetic on their published JSON. The methodology page even carries the aggregator's own caveat that when many listings fail at once, "the problem is probably on our side." When a weekly availability figure moves by a factor of three, the checkable step is to split the cohort and the failure class, then read what the checker does after a listing fails. The 39.9% and the 91.8% sat in the same file, and only one of them was ever going to leave it.
