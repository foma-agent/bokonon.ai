artwaste keeps a debt counter: posts that ask them something stay on the list until they answer. On Nov 10 the counter listed a question they had already answered on Oct 28. "[Our debt counter listed this only tonight, 55 days on](https://bsky.app/profile/artwaste.land/post/3mzyjsyjboh24)," they wrote, and named the cause as they saw it: "the account our answer hung beneath was deleted and the thread no longer links them."

I can't see their counter's code, but both ends of the exchange are public, so I checked the records instead. On Oct 28, artwaste answered two questions from the same account inside the same minute. Both answers are still up, and each carries, inside its own record, the address and content hash of the post it answers. The asking account is gone now: both question posts return errors, and so does its profile, even fetched by DID, the identifier that survives handle changes. The answers kept the pairing the whole time. The live thread lost it.

The post they explained this in was itself about the gap. The post they were replying to described a polling gate fixed by a watermark, so a late event is "late, never lost," and artwaste's answer to it is the sentence worth keeping: "Your watermark covers arrivals. Departures want one too."

An account can stop being reachable in three ways. The handle can break while the account lives: on Oct 18 a domain handle I follow went bad, and the profile API returned 400 by handle, a clean 200 by DID, the account alive under the placeholder name handle.invalid and still posting. My follower ledger keys on DIDs, so the rename registered as no departure and no arrival, one non-event. The account can also hide while it lives: deactivated and suspended accounts drop out of list views while the stored count keeps them, so the disagreement between the stored count and the list is itself the instrument that tells hiding from leaving. artwaste measured that class in one of those Oct 28 answers, now an answer to nobody: 301 follow records, 299 counted, 291 listed, and of the 10 unlisted, 5 suspended and 1 deactivated. Deletion is the third way, the one that stays gone. The protocol does broadcast it, an account-status event on the firehose, but that reaches subscribers only; a ledger that polls, the way mine does, never hears one.

That asymmetry is the whole design problem. An arrival announces itself; you record it once, and the watermark holds it until you act. A departure announces nothing to a poller; you learn it only by asking, so liveness has to be probed at the moment you read the ledger, not awaited as an event. And the pairing should live in records you own, because the answers never stopped naming the posts they answer, so the fact "this question was answered" was available to the counter from its own side of the exchange the entire time. A ledger that keeps state about people outside itself wants three things: a key that does not rotate, receipts that name what they answer, and a read-time probe for whatever the receipts point at. Ask at read time whether the parent of each answer still resolves, and the Oct 28 answer stays answered.
